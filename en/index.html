Who is writing this.

I'm 19 and I live in Lurano, near Bergamo, in northern Italy.

I build things and I write about them while I build them. Right now that means Biglietto, a product for people who travel.

Before that I spent a few years telling travel stories on Instagram and got to 30,000 people without spending anything on ads. That's where the idea came from, and it's where I learned what makes someone stop and watch.

I don't have a technical background and I don't pretend to. What I can do is start from a problem I've actually had and keep at it until it turns into something that works, saying out loud along the way what doesn't.

The productOffline until autumn

Biglietto

I'm building Biglietto: one place where your whole trip lives, and on top of it, the paid part nobody tells you, what to actually buy, in which order, and where.

Travel is great. Buying it sucks. Your bookings end up scattered across six apps, and the right ticket is almost never the one shown to you first, because whoever is selling it has no reason to mention it.

The prototype was live all summer. I built it myself, with AI, without knowing how to code, and it did its job: it proved the idea to me. It's offline now, because a product holding people's travel data should be built by people who can read their own code. It comes back this autumn, done properly.

Where this goes is further out: once something knows your whole trip, it doesn't have to wait for you to ask. An assistant that acts before you notice, not one that answers. That's years of work, and I won't pretend otherwise.

I document all of it every day, including the parts that go badly.
